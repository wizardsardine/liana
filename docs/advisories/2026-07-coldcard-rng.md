# Coldcard firmware advisory (July 2026) — rotating a key out of your Cube

On 30 July 2026 (updated 1 August) Coinkite published a security advisory about
seed generation on the Coldcard: on affected firmware, seeds were generated with
far less randomness than intended. Coincube shows a notice next to every
connected Coldcard so you can find your way here; this page is the procedure it
links to.

Sources: [Coinkite's advisory](https://blog.coinkite.com/coldcard-mk3-seed-generation-warning/)
is the authority on what is affected and which firmware fixes it. The technical
root cause is written up in [Block's engineering
analysis](https://engineering.block.xyz/blog/predictable-rng-fallback-and-32-bit-reseed-in-coldcard-firmware).

## What happened

A firmware integration error routed seed generation to a deterministic software
PRNG instead of the STM32 hardware RNG. The effect differs by model:

- **Mk2 / Mk3 on 4.0.1–4.1.9** — the worst case. For a known device state the
  seed was effectively determined rather than random.
- **Mk4 / Mk5 / Q before the fix** — the secure reseed was limited to 32 bits,
  leaving roughly 72 bits of entropy where 128 was intended.

Two exceptions come straight from the advisory:

- A seed created with **50 or more independent, private dice rolls** is not at
  risk from this issue: your dice supplied the entropy, not the broken RNG.
- A **strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase** is an independent barrier that reduces
  exposure — but it does not repair the seed underneath it. Coinkite still
  recommends migrating.

## What this means for a Cube

A Cube is a multisig wallet. **A single key cannot move funds on its own** — an
attacker who could reproduce one of your seeds still could not spend, because
your descriptor requires more than one signature. You have time to do this
properly rather than in a panic.

What does need doing: a firmware update fixes how *new* seeds are generated, but
it cannot change a seed that already exists. If a key in your Cube was generated
on affected firmware, that key should be replaced.

If the same Coldcard also holds **single-sig** wallets — outside your Cube — they
have no multisig protecting them. Deal with those first.

Coincube's behaviour with a Coldcard is unchanged: connecting, registering the
descriptor, signing PSBTs and importing xpubs all work exactly as before,
whatever the advisory says about a given device.

## Which devices are affected

Coincube reads the firmware version each Coldcard reports and shows one of two
notices:

| What the device reports | What Coincube says |
|---|---|
| Firmware inside an affected range, or a version it can't read | **Update and rotate** — update the firmware, then rotate the key |
| Firmware outside every affected range — at or past the fix, or on a track the advisory doesn't name | **Rotate recommended** — no firmware update needed; rotate if the seed predates the fix |

The second tier is not an all-clear, and Coincube never shows one. Patched
firmware says nothing about when the seed on the device was created.

Affected ranges by product line, per Coinkite's advisory:

| Line | Affected | First fixed |
|---|---|---|
| Mk2 / Mk3 | `4.0.1` – `4.1.9` inclusive | `4.2.0` |
| Mk4 / Mk5 | below `5.6.0` | `5.6.0` |
| Mk4 / Mk5 (Edge) | below `6.6.0X` | `6.6.0X` |
| Q | below `1.5.0Q` | `1.5.0Q` |
| Q (Edge) | below `6.6.0QX` | `6.6.0QX` |

Coincube treats **`4.0.0` as affected too**. Coinkite's advisory names `4.0.1` as
the first affected release; [Block's
analysis](https://engineering.block.xyz/blog/predictable-rng-fallback-and-32-bit-reseed-in-coldcard-firmware)
of the same bug puts it at `4.0.0`. Coincube takes the wider bound, so a device
on `4.0.0` gets the **Update and rotate** notice — over-warning is the safe
direction, and the procedure below is the same either way.

A Coldcard Q on mainline firmware reports a version string (`1.5.0Q`) that isn't
valid semver, so Coincube can't read it and shows the stronger notice. That is
the intended behaviour for any unreadable version.

## Rotating the key

Every step below uses a flow Coincube already has. Nothing here is specific to
this advisory.

### 1. Update the device firmware

Follow [Coinkite's upgrade instructions](https://coldcard.com/docs/upgrade/) for
your model, and confirm the fixed version from the table above is installed.
Updating the firmware does not change the seed on the device — it is the
prerequisite for step 2, not the fix for an existing key.

### 2. Generate a **new** seed on the device

On the Coldcard, create a new wallet seed and write down its backup words. Do
**not** restore the old seed onto the updated firmware: the point of the
exercise is a seed generated by fixed firmware. The device now has a new master
fingerprint — this is what makes it a different key as far as your descriptor is
concerned.

### 3. Rotate the key out of your Cube

In Coincube, edit the Cube's descriptor and replace the old Coldcard key with
the new one:

- Open the Cube, go to **Settings → Recreate/edit the descriptor** (the same
  editor used when the Cube was created).
- In the spending path holding the old Coldcard key, select that key and choose
  the device again. With the new seed on it, the Coldcard now offers the new
  xpub.
- Apply, and finish the descriptor flow. Coincube writes a **new descriptor** —
  the old one is not modified, and the coins do not move by themselves.
- Register the new descriptor on every signing device in the Cube, Coldcard
  included (**Settings → Signing devices → Register wallet**). Devices that
  don't know the descriptor cannot sign for it.

Keep the old descriptor. Until step 4 is complete and confirmed, the funds still
live under it and you need every one of its keys to move them.

### 4. Sweep the funds to the new descriptor

The new descriptor is a different wallet — rotating a key does not move coins.
Send the balance from the old Cube to a receive address of the new one:

- Get a receive address from the new Cube (**Receive**), and verify it on a
  device.
- From the old Cube, create a spend for the full balance to that address
  (**Send**, then "send max"), and sign it with enough keys of the *old*
  descriptor — the old Coldcard key included, if that path needs it.
- Broadcast, and wait for confirmation.

If the Cube holds a lot, do a small test spend first and confirm it arrives
before sweeping the rest.

### 5. Verify

- The new Cube shows the swept balance, confirmed.
- The old Cube's balance is zero.
- Every device in the new Cube has the new descriptor registered, and you have
  the new seed backup written down and stored the way you store the others.
- Coincube still shows the advisory badge on the Coldcard. That is expected:
  the badge marks the device, not the key, and no firmware version can prove
  when a seed was created. You can collapse the notice from the device list
  once you've acted on it.
